How to read a warning light

Choose a light to see what it usually means.

Amber light: get it checked soon

Check engine

What it usually means
The engine computer has found a fault. It can be small, like a loose fuel cap, or something that needs fixing, like a sensor or ignition problem.
Can I keep driving?
If it stays on steadily, you can usually drive on gently and book it in soon. If it flashes, the engine may be misfiring badly: slow down, stop somewhere safe as soon as you can and call, as driving on can damage the exhaust system. Your owner's manual has the details for your car.

All mechanical repairs (engine).

Red light: act now

Battery or charging

What it usually means
The battery isn't being charged. Common causes are a worn alternator, a loose or broken drive belt, or a battery or wiring fault.
Can I keep driving?
Not far. The car is running on what's left in the battery and may stop. Switch off what you don't need, like the air con and radio, and get somewhere safe. If other lights come on too, stop.
Who handles it at Shine
Batteries, and all mechanical repairs for the charging system.

Red light: stop safely

Oil pressure

What it usually means
Oil pressure is too low. The engine may be low on oil, or there may be a fault with the oil pump or a sensor.
Can I keep driving?
No. Stop as soon as it's safe and switch the engine off. An engine without oil pressure can be damaged quickly. Check the oil level once it has cooled, and call before driving on.
Who handles it at Shine
All mechanical repairs, and logbook servicing to keep the oil changed on time.

Red light: stop safely

Brake system

What it usually means
On most cars it lights up when the park brake is on. If it stays on with the park brake off, the brake fluid may be low or there may be a fault in the brakes.
Can I keep driving?
Check the park brake first. If the light stays on, stop somewhere safe and don't drive on until the brakes have been checked.
Who handles it at Shine
All mechanical repairs (brakes).

Amber light: get it checked soon

ABS

What it usually means
The anti-lock braking system has a fault. Your normal brakes should still work, but the wheels can lock under hard braking.
Can I keep driving?
Usually yes, carefully. Leave extra room in front and book it in soon. If the red brake light is on as well, stop safely.
Who handles it at Shine
All mechanical repairs (brakes).

Amber light: get it checked soon

Tyre pressure

What it usually means
One or more tyres is below its set pressure. Only cars with tyre pressure monitoring have this light.
Can I keep driving?
Gently, to the nearest air pump. Inflate the tyres to the pressures on the tyre placard, often inside the driver's door. If a tyre looks flat or the car pulls to one side, stop somewhere safe.
Who handles it at Shine
Tyres.

Red on many cars: don't put it off

Airbag (SRS)

What it usually means
The airbag system has found a fault. The airbags, and on some cars the seatbelt pretensioners, may not work in a crash.
Can I keep driving?
The car will usually drive normally, but you and your passengers are less protected. Have it checked soon.

Red light: stop safely

Engine temperature

What it usually means
The engine is running too hot. Common causes are low coolant, a leak, or a fault with the fan, thermostat or water pump.
Can I keep driving?
No. Stop as soon as it's safe and switch off. Let the engine cool, and never open the radiator cap while it's hot. Call before driving on.

All mechanical repairs (engine).

Something you notice, not a warning light

Air con not cooling

What it usually means
The system may be low on gas from a slow leak, or a part such as the compressor or a fan may have a fault.
Can I keep driving?
Yes. The air con also helps clear a fogged windscreen, so take more care in wet weather until it's fixed.

Air con regas and repairs.

What a pink slip checks, and how to pass first time

Pink slip check

Tyres

Tread depth, cuts, bulges and damage, and matching tyres on each axle.

Check at homeTread should be at least 1.5mm, level with the wear bars in the grooves. Look for cuts or bulges in the sidewalls.

Pink slip check

Brakes

That the car pulls up straight, and the brakes and handbrake work as they should.

Check at homeNotice if the car pulls to one side when you brake. A brake or ABS warning light that stays on is a reason to fail.

Pink slip check

Steering and suspension

Play in the steering, worn joints, leaking shocks and anything else that affects how the car handles.

Check at homeListen for clunks over bumps, and notice any looseness or wander in the steering.

Pink slip check

Lights and indicators

Headlights, park, tail, brake, indicator and number plate lights all working, with lenses intact.

Check at homeAsk someone to watch while you press the brake and use each indicator. A blown globe is an easy fail to avoid.

Pink slip check

Windscreen and wipers

Cracks or chips in the driver's view, and wipers that clear the glass.

Check at homeCheck the glass in front of the driver for chips or cracks, and replace wiper blades that smear.

Pink slip check

Seatbelts

Every belt latches, retracts and isn't frayed or cut.

Check at homePull each belt out fully, buckle it and let it go. It should latch and wind back in cleanly.

Pink slip check

Body and rust

Rust or damage that weakens the body or leaves sharp edges.

Check at homeLook along the door sills and wheel arches for rust holes.

Pink slip check

Exhaust

Leaks, loose mounts and where the pipe comes out.

Check at homeA louder exhaust or a blowing sound can point to a leak.

Pink slip check

LPG system

On LPG cars, the tank, lines and fittings.

Check at homeLPG tanks must be checked every 10 years. Mention the tank's date when you book.
