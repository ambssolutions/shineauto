How to read a warning light

Choose a light to see what it usually means. As a rule of thumb:

  • Red usually means stop when it's safe, or act now.
  • Amber usually means get it checked soon.

If a light comes on with a noise, smell, smoke or the car feels different, pull over somewhere safe and call.

Amber light: get it checked soon

Check engine

What it usually means
The engine computer has found a fault. It can be small, like a loose fuel cap, or something that needs fixing, like a sensor or ignition problem.
Can I keep driving?
If it stays on steadily, you can usually drive on gently and book it in soon. If it flashes, the engine may be misfiring badly: slow down, stop somewhere safe as soon as you can and call, as driving on can damage the exhaust system. Your owner's manual has the details for your car.

Red light: act now

Battery or charging

What it usually means
The battery isn't being charged. Common causes are a worn alternator, a loose or broken drive belt, or a battery or wiring fault.
Can I keep driving?
Not far. The car is running on what's left in the battery and may stop. Switch off what you don't need, like the air con and radio, and get somewhere safe. If other lights come on too, stop.
Who handles it at Shine

Red light: stop safely

Oil pressure

What it usually means
Oil pressure is too low. The engine may be low on oil, or there may be a fault with the oil pump or a sensor.
Can I keep driving?
No. Stop as soon as it's safe and switch the engine off. An engine without oil pressure can be damaged quickly. Check the oil level once it has cooled, and call before driving on.

Brake system

What it usually means
On most cars it lights up when the park brake is on. If it stays on with the park brake off, the brake fluid may be low or there may be a fault in the brakes.
Can I keep driving?
Check the park brake first. If the light stays on, stop somewhere safe and don't drive on until the brakes have been checked.

Amber light: get it checked soon

ABS

What it usually means
The anti-lock braking system has a fault. Your normal brakes should still work, but the wheels can lock under hard braking.
Can I keep driving?
Usually yes, carefully. Leave extra room in front and book it in soon. If the red brake light is on as well, stop safely.

Amber light: get it checked soon

Tyre pressure

What it usually means
One or more tyres is below its set pressure. Only cars with tyre pressure monitoring have this light.
Can I keep driving?
Gently, to the nearest air pump. Inflate the tyres to the pressures on the tyre placard, often inside the driver's door. If a tyre looks flat or the car pulls to one side, stop somewhere safe.

Red light: stop safely

Engine temperature

What it usually means
The engine is running too hot. Common causes are low coolant, a leak, or a fault with the fan, thermostat or water pump.
Can I keep driving?
No. Stop as soon as it's safe and switch off. Let the engine cool, and never open the radiator cap while it's hot. Call before driving on.

Something you notice, not a warning light

Air con not cooling

What it usually means
The system may be low on gas from a slow leak, or a part such as the compressor or a fan may have a fault.
Can I keep driving?
Yes. The air con also helps clear a fogged windscreen, so take more care in wet weather until it's fixed.
