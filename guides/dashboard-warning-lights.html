Dashboard warning lights explained: what to do when one comes on

Red warning lights usually mean stop when it's safe, or act now. Amber lights usually mean get it checked soon. The oil, temperature and brake lights are the ones to stop for straight away. A steady check engine light usually means book it in soon.

By Shine Auto Repairs, Warwick Farm15 November 20265 min read

This is general guidance only. Cars differ, so check your owner's manual for what each light means on your car. If a light comes on with a noise, smell, smoke or the car feels different, pull over somewhere safe and call.

Red or amber: how to read the colour

  • Red usually means stop when it's safe, or act now.
  • Amber usually means get it checked soon.

Most lights also flash on for a moment when you start the car. That's the car checking its own bulbs and systems. It's only a warning if the light stays on, comes back on while driving, or flashes.

The lights you'll see most often

Check engine light

Amber light: get it checked soon

What it usually means
The engine computer has found a fault. It can be small, like a loose fuel cap, or something that needs fixing, like a sensor or ignition problem.
Can I keep driving?
If it stays on steadily, you can usually drive on gently and book it in soon. If it flashes, the engine may be misfiring badly: slow down, stop somewhere safe as soon as you can and call, as driving on can damage the exhaust system. Your owner's manual has the details for your car.

Oil pressure light

Red light: stop safely

What it usually means
Oil pressure is too low. The engine may be low on oil, or there may be a fault with the oil pump or a sensor.
Can I keep driving?
No. Stop as soon as it's safe and switch the engine off. An engine without oil pressure can be damaged quickly. Check the oil level once it has cooled, and call before driving on.

Battery or charging light

Red light: act now

What it usually means
The battery isn't being charged. Common causes are a worn alternator, a loose or broken drive belt, or a battery or wiring fault.
Can I keep driving?
Not far. The car is running on what's left in the battery and may stop. Switch off what you don't need, like the air con and radio, and get somewhere safe. If other lights come on too, stop.

Brake system light

Red light: stop safely

What it usually means
On most cars it lights up when the park brake is on. If it stays on with the park brake off, the brake fluid may be low or there may be a fault in the brakes.
Can I keep driving?
Check the park brake first. If the light stays on, stop somewhere safe and don't drive on until the brakes have been checked.

ABS light

Amber light: get it checked soon

What it usually means
The anti-lock braking system has a fault. Your normal brakes should still work, but the wheels can lock under hard braking.
Can I keep driving?
Usually yes, carefully. Leave extra room in front and book it in soon. If the red brake light is on as well, stop safely.

Engine temperature light

Red light: stop safely

What it usually means
The engine is running too hot. Common causes are low coolant, a leak, or a fault with the fan, thermostat or water pump.
Can I keep driving?
No. Stop as soon as it's safe and switch off. Let the engine cool, and never open the radiator cap while it's hot. Call before driving on.

Tyre pressure light

Amber light: get it checked soon

What it usually means
One or more tyres is below its set pressure. Only cars with tyre pressure monitoring have this light.
Can I keep driving?
Gently, to the nearest air pump. Inflate the tyres to the pressures on the tyre placard, often inside the driver's door. If a tyre looks flat or the car pulls to one side, stop somewhere safe.

Airbag (SRS) light

Red on many cars: don't put it off

What it usually means
The airbag system has found a fault. The airbags, and on some cars the seatbelt pretensioners, may not work in a crash.
Can I keep driving?
The car will usually drive normally, but you and your passengers are less protected. Have it checked soon.

What to do when a light comes on

  1. Note the colour and whether it's steady or flashing. Red or flashing means find somewhere safe to stop.
  2. Notice how the car feels. Any new noise, smell, smoke, loss of power or change in the brakes or steering means stop.
  3. Check the simple things once you're stopped. The park brake for the brake light, the oil level once the engine has cooled, the fuel cap for a check engine light.
  4. Look it up in the owner's manual, which shows every light for your model.
  5. Call before driving on if it's a red light or you're unsure. Describe what the light is doing and we'll tell you what to do next.

Warning lights and your pink slip

Some lights matter at rego time too. Under the NSW safety check standards, a brake or ABS warning light that stays on with the engine running is a reason to fail, and so is an airbag light that doesn't go out after start-up. If one of these has been on for a while, get it fixed before your pink slip.

Common questions

Can I drive with the check engine light on?

If it's steady, you can usually drive on gently and book it in soon. If it's flashing, slow down, stop somewhere safe as soon as you can and call, as the engine may be misfiring badly.

What should I do if the oil light comes on?

Stop as soon as it's safe and switch the engine off. Check the oil level once it has cooled, and call before driving on.

Why is my battery light on while I'm driving?

It usually means the battery isn't being charged. The car may not run for long, so switch off what you don't need and get somewhere safe.

Will a warning light fail my pink slip?

A brake or ABS light that stays on, or an airbag light that doesn't go out after start-up, is a reason to fail under the NSW safety check standards.
