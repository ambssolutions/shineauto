What does a pink slip check? A plain-English NSW checklist

A NSW pink slip checks that your car is safe to drive: brakes, steering and suspension, wheels and tyres, the body and glass, seats and seatbelts, lights, and leaks from the engine, exhaust and fuel system. It doesn't test how well the engine runs or how the car looks.

By Shine Auto Repairs, Warwick Farm15 November 20265 min read

Examiners work from Transport for NSW's Light Vehicle Safety Check Standards, part of the Authorised Inspection Scheme (AIS) rules. You may also see the pink slip called an eSafety check. Below is what those standards cover for a car, ute or light four-wheel drive, written the way we'd explain it at the counter.

Quick answer

The check covers brakes, steering and suspension, wheels and tyres, body and glass, seats and seatbelts, lights, and leaks. The most common fails are simple things: worn tyres, a light that doesn't work, perished wiper blades, a warning light that stays on, or an oil leak dripping onto the road.

Brakes

  • The brake pedal and handbrake work properly, and the pedal doesn't sink to the floor under steady pressure.
  • Brake parts you can see aren't leaking and are securely mounted.
  • A brake test shows the car stops hard enough and in a straight line.
  • The brake and ABS warning lights come on when you turn the key and go out once the engine is running. A brake or ABS light that stays on is a reason to fail.

Steering and suspension

  • The steering wheel is secure and doesn't have too much free play.
  • Steering parts under the bonnet and under the car are all there, tight, and not worn past the limits.
  • Suspension parts aren't broken, cracked or loose, shock absorbers and struts work and are securely mounted, and the wheels don't have too much play when raised.

Wheels and tyres

  • Every road tyre has at least 1.5mm of tread on the surfaces that touch the road.
  • No deep cuts, bulges or exposed cords.
  • Wheels aren't damaged, wheel nuts are all there, and nothing rubs on the tyres when you steer or go over bumps.

Tyres are one of the easiest fails to see coming. Our summer car check explains how to read your tread.

Body, glass and seats

  • Doors open, close and latch properly from inside and out.
  • The windscreen in front of the driver isn't cracked or badly damaged. Window tint isn't darker than the rules allow.
  • No rusted sharp edges on the outside, and no advanced rust or cracks in structural areas such as seat and seatbelt mounts.
  • Rear vision mirrors are fitted and give a clear view.
  • Seats are secure, and every seatbelt is there, isn't knotted, locks into its buckle and has a working retractor.
  • Wipers and blades are in good condition, the horn works and the demister blows air onto the windscreen.
  • If the car has airbags, the airbag warning light comes on at start-up and then goes out.

Lights

  • Headlights on high and low beam, park lights, tail lights, brake lights, indicators and the number plate light all work.
  • Lenses aren't broken so that white light shows where it shouldn't, and there are no tinted covers over lights.
  • Headlights are aimed correctly and the reflectors aren't badly tarnished.
  • The rear reflectors are fitted and undamaged.

Engine, exhaust and fuel

  • No oil leaking from the engine, gearbox or driveline onto the road, the exhaust or the brakes.
  • The exhaust is securely mounted, doesn't leak, and comes out behind the rear passenger doors.
  • The fuel system doesn't leak.
  • On LPG cars, the gas system gets its own set of checks. We cover those in pink slips for LPG and diesel cars.

What commonly fails a pink slip

Plenty of fails aren't big mechanical problems. They're often small things that are easy to miss when you drive the car every day:

  1. tyres worn below 1.5mm, or worn unevenly on the inside edge where you can't see it
  2. a blown globe, often a number plate light or a single brake light
  3. split or smearing wiper blades
  4. a brake, ABS or airbag warning light that stays on
  5. an oil leak heavy enough to drip
  6. a crack in the windscreen in the driver's view
  7. a seatbelt that won't retract or latch.

A five-minute walk around the car before you book can save a second trip. Turn on every light and get someone to stand behind the car while you press the brake.

What a pink slip doesn't check

It's a safety check, not a full service or a pre-purchase inspection. It won't tell you when the timing belt is due, how healthy the engine is or whether the air con works. For that, a logbook service or a repair inspection is the better fit.

Common questions

What is the minimum tyre tread for a pink slip in NSW?

The NSW safety check standards reject a tyre with less than 1.5mm of tread on the surfaces that normally touch the road.

Will a check engine light fail a pink slip?

The standards list the brake, ABS and airbag warning lights. A check engine light isn't on that list, but it's still worth having looked at, as the fault behind it can lead to something that does fail, such as a leak.

Is an eSafety check the same as a pink slip?

Yes. eSafety check is another name you'll see for the NSW pink slip, the safety inspection report most cars over five years old need to renew rego.

Can a cracked windscreen fail a pink slip?

It can. Cracks or damage in the area in front of the driver are a reason for rejection. Small repaired chips may be accepted if they're within the limits.
