Logbook servicing: what it is and why it matters

A logbook service is a service done to the schedule your car's maker sets out in the logbook or owner's manual. Each one is due at a set distance or time, whichever comes first, and covers the work the maker lists for that interval.

By Shine Auto Repairs, Warwick Farm15 November 20264 min read

Quick answer

Your car's maker lists what should be done at each service and when, usually every so many kilometres or months. A logbook service follows that list for your car's interval, and the service is recorded in the logbook so there's a history of the work.

What the logbook is

Most cars come with a service logbook, either a printed book in the glovebox or an online record. It sets out the service schedule for your model: what's done at each interval and the distance or time each one falls due.

The schedule is written for your exact car. A small petrol hatch and a turbo-diesel ute have very different lists, so there's no single "standard service". That's the point of following the logbook rather than a generic oil change.

What's usually included

It depends on the interval, but a logbook service usually includes some of these:

  • engine oil and oil filter change, with the grade the maker specifies
  • checking and topping up fluids, such as coolant, brake fluid and washer fluid
  • air filter, cabin filter and fuel filter, at the intervals listed
  • a check of the brakes, tyres, steering, suspension, lights, wipers and battery
  • bigger scheduled items at certain intervals, such as spark plugs, brake fluid replacement, coolant replacement or a timing belt
  • a record of the service in the logbook and, where the car has one, the service reminder reset.

If the inspection finds something outside the schedule, like worn brake pads or a leaking shock absorber, ask the workshop to tell you what it found, what it costs and how urgent it is before any extra work is done.

How to tell when your service is due

  • A service reminder on the dash, often a spanner symbol or a message.
  • The windscreen sticker from your last service, showing the date or distance for the next one.
  • The logbook itself. Find the last stamped entry and look up the next interval.

Most schedules say "whichever comes first". If you don't drive much, the time limit can come up well before the kilometres. Oil and fluids age even when the car is parked.

Why it matters

It catches wear early

Each service includes a look over the car. Worn tyres, a weeping water pump or a perished drive belt are cheaper to fix when they're found on the hoist than when they let go on the M5.

It keeps the big jobs on time

Some parts, like timing belts on engines that have them, are replaced at set intervals in the schedule. Missing one can mean serious engine damage. The logbook is how you know it's due.

It builds a service history

A full, stamped logbook shows the next owner the car has been looked after. Buyers ask about it, and it can make a car easier to sell.

It helps at rego time

A car that's serviced on time is less likely to fail its pink slip on brakes, tyres, wipers or leaks, because those get looked at along the way.

Common questions

What is a logbook service?

It's a service done to the schedule set by your car's maker in the logbook or owner's manual, covering the items listed for that interval, and recorded in the logbook.

How often do I need a logbook service?

It depends on your car. The logbook gives each interval as a distance or a time, whichever comes first. Check the last stamped entry or your windscreen sticker.

What should I bring to a logbook service?

Bring your service logbook, or the details of your online logbook, so the right interval can be followed and the service recorded.

Is a logbook service the same as a pink slip?

No. A pink slip is a NSW safety check for rego. A logbook service is maintenance to your maker's schedule. Many people book them together.
